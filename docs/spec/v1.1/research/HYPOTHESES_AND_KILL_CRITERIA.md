# Hypotheses and Kill Criteria

These are research hypotheses, not architecture assumptions.

## H1 — persistent structure

At sufficiently complex/long strategic-network investigations, the structured investigator will outperform strong monolithic frontier-model baselines on final access-relative material conclusions and at least one other persistent-state capability such as true-world retention or recovery after disconfirmation.

### Falsifying/negative result

No repeatable advantage appears at the predeclared non-smoke scale once the baseline is strong, compute-matched, given identical visible evidence, and allowed iterative self-critique.

A small-scale negative result alone does not falsify a scale-dependent version of H1; that version remains `NOT_TESTED` until an appropriate larger scale is run.

## H2 — cheap local evaluation and search breadth

A cheaper bounded evaluator may permit more local judgments/world exploration under a fixed resource budget, and that additional breadth may improve final investigation quality.

### Counter-hypothesis

Cheaper/noisier judgments increase false-fit survival and broader search makes results worse.

Both outcomes are scientifically acceptable.

## H3 — candidate generation bottleneck

As environment complexity grows, omission of the true mechanism from the candidate set will explain a substantial fraction of end-to-end failure.

### Diagnostic

Generation-oracle injection materially improves performance while local-evaluator oracle does not close the same gap.

## H4 — access-sensitive epistemic behavior

The investigator should remain appropriately unresolved under restricted access and become more decisive only when newly revealed evidence actually separates worlds.

This includes both sides of the error: premature resolution and pathological over-hedging.

## Project kill conditions

Stop or radically rethink the initial architecture if any of the following holds after reproducible runs at the relevant tested scale:

- the structured system offers no end-to-end/persistent-state advantage over the compute-matched monolithic baseline;
- true-world omission is so severe that the loop mostly evaluates irrelevant candidates and attack/reframing does not repair it;
- local evaluator errors dominate even with frontier models, while oracle local judgments succeed;
- the ordinal aggregator systematically rewards false worlds on oracle-labeled simulator cases;
- the simulator is only solvable because the investigator learns template artifacts;
- cost/latency makes the structured loop impractical and cheaper evaluators do not preserve quality;
- results depend primarily on benchmark-specific ontology choices rather than investigation behavior;
- the early monolithic baseline already performs near ceiling at the largest feasible intended scale, leaving little research headroom for structure.
